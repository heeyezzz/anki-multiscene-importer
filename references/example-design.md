# Variable-retrieval example design

Use the five `Sentence` fields to create different, useful conditions for retrieving the same word. More examples are not inherently better: each one must add a meaningful dimension of use.

## Required qualities

1. **Strongly distinct situations.** Do not merely swap a subject, location, or similar vehicle. Use genuinely different domains or communicative purposes, such as a travel notice, customer email, meeting, everyday conversation, or news report. A `Scene` label describes that distinct situation in short Chinese.
2. **Precise, non-spoiling clues.** The surrounding words should make the target word or target form the clearly best answer, but must not state its definition or give away the exact answer. Avoid frames such as `It was a long ____` where many unrelated words fit.
3. **Low extraneous difficulty.** Apart from the target, use familiar vocabulary and straightforward grammar. The card should test retrieval of the target word, not become a reading-comprehension puzzle caused by several new words or dense clauses.
4. **Useful variation in use.** Across five examples, deliberately vary high-frequency meanings, collocations, grammatical forms, or sentence functions when those variations are real and common. For example, a word may appear in a frequent noun phrase, a verb pattern, a passive form, or a high-frequency fixed expression. Do not introduce rare or specialist meanings just to make all five examples different.
5. **Fast to review.** Prefer one natural sentence of roughly 8–15 English words. A slightly longer sentence is acceptable only when its extra context makes the answer genuinely more inferable.

## Selection order

Prioritize high-frequency, natural usage before breadth. For polysemous words, cover the central sense or senses first; add a less common sense only when it is common enough to repay the additional interference. The five examples should vary one or more meaningful language dimensions—situation, collocation, sense, grammatical form, or sentence function—not surface wording alone.

## Final review checklist

Before importing, verify all five examples:

- sound natural in the stated scene;
- are recognizably different scenes or uses;
- contain enough contextual evidence to infer the clozed form;
- keep the target word as the main difficulty;
- collectively teach a useful, high-frequency usage network; and
- avoid repeating the same answer frame or collocation without a learning reason.
